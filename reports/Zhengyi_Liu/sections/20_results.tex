\section{Methods and results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Numerical protocol and validation}
\ai{The project's Block~3 implementation uses Qiskit statevector/Aer
simulation~\cite{majoranaMachine2026,qiskit2024}.  ED serves only as a
small-$L$ certificate; VQE prepares the states plotted below.  Table~\ref{tab:settings} lists the production settings.}

\begin{table}[H]
 \centering
 \small
 \begin{tabular}{ll}
  \toprule
  quantity & value \\
  \midrule
  chain and couplings & $L=4$, $t=\Delta=1$ \\
  sweep & $81$ points, $\mu/t\in[-3.5,3.5]$ \\
  ansatz & $R_Y$ + linear CNOT, $r=3$, $N_\theta=16$ \\
  optimizer & COBYLA, at most $500$ iterations per start \\
  symmetry penalty & $\lambda(1-\langle P\rangle)^2$, $\lambda=3$ \\
  continuation & previous optimum as the next initial point \\
  recovery & up to $4$ additional random restarts \\
  readout & $4096$ shots for $O_{\mathrm{edge}}$ \\
  acceptance & $|\Delta E|\le0.05$, $|1-\langle P\rangle|\le0.01$, $\mathcal F\ge0.99$ \\
  \bottomrule
 \end{tabular}
 \caption{\ai{Settings of the parity-constrained VQE sweep.}}
 \label{tab:settings}
\end{table}

\ai{The sweep is warm-started: $\boldsymbol\theta^*(\mu_k)$ initializes the
optimization at the neighbouring point $\mu_{k+1}$.  If a point fails any of the
three acceptance tests, random restarts are evaluated.  For this validation
study the final candidate is selected by its fidelity to the ED even-sector
ground state.  This ED-assisted post-selection is deliberately stringent for a
benchmark, but it is not a device-only selection rule and should not be confused
with an experimentally deployable VQE stopping criterion.}

\subsection{Local versus non-local readout}
\ai{Figure~\ref{fig:local_nonlocal} gives the first operational check.  The local
parity-odd observable is statistically compatible with zero in all three
optimized states, consistent with the parity argument in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_zero}.  The non-local string instead separates
representative regimes: its magnitude is about $0.99$ at $\mu=0$,
approximately $0.24$ at the bulk-critical parameter $\mu=2t$, and about $0.11$
at $\mu=3t$.  This representative-point test uses $8192$ shots per observable;
the full continuation below uses $4096$.  The result is not merely ``measure an
edge qubit'': both endpoints and the Jordan--Wigner $Z$ string are essential.}

 \caption{\ai{Shot-based VQE measurement of a local parity-odd operator and the
 non-local edge string for topological, critical, and trivial representative
 points.  Error bars show finite-shot uncertainty.}}
 \label{fig:local_nonlocal}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Parity-constrained VQE across the phase sweep}
\ai{The central result is Fig.~\ref{fig:vqe_sweep}.  The ED reference, noiseless
VQE expectation, and the $4096$-shot estimator lie on the same finite-size
crossover.  The signal reaches essentially one at the sweet spot $\mu=0$, falls
to about $0.23$ at $|\mu|=2t$, and approaches roughly $0.05$ by
$|\mu/t|=3.5$.  A four-site system cannot display a discontinuous thermodynamic
transition; the dashed lines therefore mark the known bulk boundary while the
measured string changes smoothly around it.  Shot fluctuations remain
consistent with the $N^{-1/2}\simeq0.016$ worst-case scale and become smaller
near unit expectation.}

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{block3_week7_vqe_sweep.pdf}
 \caption{\ai{Top: ED, ideal VQE, and finite-shot edge-string curves across the
 chemical-potential sweep.  Bottom: energy error, infidelity, and parity
 deviation used to validate every prepared state.}}
 \label{fig:vqe_sweep}
\end{figure}

\ai{The lower panel is important because agreement of one observable alone could
be accidental.  In the displayed run the energy error stays below
$3\times10^{-2}$, the infidelity below approximately $5\times10^{-3}$, and the
parity deviation below $10^{-3}$, all within the acceptance thresholds in
Table~\ref{tab:settings}.  The larger errors at the first, negative-$\mu$ end of
the curve are an optimization-history effect: the first point starts randomly,
while later points benefit from continuation.  They should not be interpreted as
a physical $\mu\leftrightarrow-\mu$ asymmetry.}

\subsection{How deep must the noiseless ansatz be?}
\ai{The depth scan in Fig.~\ref{fig:depth} repeats the optimization with six
random starts at $\mu/t=0,2,3$ and chooses the ED-validated best state at each
depth.  One repetition is under-expressive: the infidelity is of order unity in
the topological and critical cases and remains just above the $10^{-2}$ target
in the trivial case.  At $r=2$ all three representative points cross below the
fidelity threshold; additional repetitions remain accurate but do not improve
monotonically because the classical optimization itself is stochastic and
non-convex.  Choosing $r=3$ for the full sweep is therefore conservative: it
adds one layer beyond the observed noiseless expressibility threshold without
making the circuit unnecessarily deep.  The separate question of how gate noise
changes this choice belongs to Part~5.}

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{block3_week7_depth_fidelity.pdf}
 \caption{\ai{Noiseless ansatz-depth diagnostic at representative topological,
 critical, and trivial points.  Two repetitions are already sufficient for the
 $L=4$ fidelity target; $r=3$ is used in the full sweep.}}
 \label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

\ai{The ideal Block~3 workflow therefore closes the measurement loop: a
gate-based VQE state, symmetry control, basis-rotated bitstring processing, and
a non-local observable reproduce the expected finite-size phase diagnostic.
Its principal limitations are equally clear: the demonstration uses only
$L=4$, relies on ED for validation and restart ranking, and contains sampling
noise but no physical gate or decoherence channel.}
