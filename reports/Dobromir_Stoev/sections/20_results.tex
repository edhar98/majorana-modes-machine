\section{Methods and Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Circuit-level implementation and verification}

\ai{All circuit results use $L=4$, $t=\Delta=1$, the same parity-constrained VQE
and edge string as Part 4, and a linear \texttt{RY}+\texttt{cx}
\texttt{EfficientSU2} ansatz~\cite{peruzzo2014vqe}.  Circuits are transpiled at
optimization level zero to the fixed basis
\texttt{[rz,sx,cx]}, ensuring that an error registered on \texttt{cx} fires after
every entangling instruction.  The state is saved as a density matrix and the
diagnostic is evaluated as $\operatorname{Tr}(O_{\mathrm{edge}}\rho)$.}

\ai{The integration was verified independently.  A reference routine evolves
the density matrix gate by gate with \texttt{Operator} and explicitly applies a
depolarizing \texttt{SuperOp} after each CNOT; it does not use Aer's noise
machinery.  Across 41 values of $\mu/t$, the reference and Aer results agree at
approximately $10^{-15}$.  Figure~\ref{fig:verification} also compares the
deliberately strong $p_{\mathrm{cx}}=0.10$ depolarizing channel with thermal
relaxation at $T_1=80\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, $T_2=60\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{cx}}=300\,\mathrm{ns}$.  The associated one-gate relaxation
probability is only $1-e^{-\tau/T_1}\simeq0.0037$, so the thermal curve remains
closer to the ideal one.}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{block4_week9_verification.pdf}
  \caption{Left: the Aer \texttt{NoiseModel} edge string and the independent
  exact gate-by-gate reference agree to $\sim10^{-15}$ across the $\mu$ sweep.
  Right: depolarizing versus thermal $T_1/T_2$ noise at matched gate placement.}
  \label{fig:verification}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The optimal depth is an expressibility threshold}

\ai{At the topological sweet spot $\mu=0$, the noiseless edge string is only
$\simeq0.004$ for $r=1$ but jumps to approximately one at $r=2$ and remains
saturated for deeper circuits.  Thus the failure at $r=1$ is under-expressibility,
not an optimizer accident.  With $p_{\mathrm{cx}}=0.05$, the signal peaks at
$r^*=2$; every further repetition adds three noisy CNOTs without an ideal-state
gain.  Figure~\ref{fig:depth} makes the two factors in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:depth_tradeoff} visible separately.}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{block4_week9_depth_optimum.pdf}
  \caption{For $L=4$, the noiseless edge string has an expressibility threshold:
  $r=1$ fails, $r=2$ succeeds, and deeper circuits mainly accumulate CNOT noise.}
  \label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

\ai{Parameter perturbations provide a complementary diagnostic.  Gaussian
$\delta\theta$ rotates the ideal state away from the optimum while its purity
stays at one.  Per-CNOT depolarization suppresses the same edge string while the
purity falls.  The two errors should therefore not be summarized by a single
``effective gate error'': coherent drift is in principle recalibratable, whereas
incoherent loss cannot be undone by retuning one angle vector.}

\subsection{Noisy optimization and backend calibration}

\ai{The noisy-cost experiment uses $L=4$, $r=3$, $\mu=0$, $\lambda=0.1$ and
COBYLA, warm-started from the noiseless optimum with additional random starts.
Depolarizing noise is unital and produces almost no exploitable displacement of
the optimum.  Amplitude damping is non-unital: re-optimization can increase the
\emph{measured} edge string while moving the underlying noiseless circuit away
from the exact ground state.  Selected values from the reproducible seed-7 sweep
are listed in Table~\ref{tab:noisy}.}

\ai{Finally, a \texttt{NoiseModel.from\_backend} calculation replaces the
uniform toy channel with the recorded \texttt{FakeManilaV2} calibration.  Its
heterogeneous CNOT error rates are about $0.009$--$0.014$.  At $\mu=0$ the ideal,
backend-calibrated, and uniform $p_{\mathrm{cx}}=0.05$ edge strings are
$0.99$, $0.87$, and $0.67$, respectively.  The uniform model is therefore a
conservative caricature rather than a calibrated prediction.  This comparison
is still mildly optimistic: the backend noise model contains readout errors, but
they do not fire because the simulation reads
$\operatorname{Tr}(O_{\mathrm{edge}}\rho)$ directly without measurement
instructions.  The calculation uses a recorded calibration snapshot, not a live
hardware run.}

\ai{Together these tests show that realistic noise primarily reduces the
contrast of the non-local diagnostic.  Circuit depth should be chosen by
noiseless expressibility validation followed by noisy evaluation, and any
noise-aware optimization must report a state-quality check rather than only the
observable it was able to improve.  These conclusions concern the $L=4$ NISQ
circuit; their large-$L$ consequences are treated separately in Part 6.}
