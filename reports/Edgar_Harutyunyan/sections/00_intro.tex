\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The \href{https://github.com/edhar98/majorana-modes-machine}{\emph{Majorana Modes in the Machine}} project follows the Kitaev chain from
its theoretical description to its simulation on noisy quantum circuits.
G.~Schewtschik's report, \href{https://edhar98.github.io/majorana-modes-machine/reports/Guilherme_Schewtschik/report.pdf}{\emph{Kitaev Chain \& Bulk Topology}}, introduces
the bulk Hamiltonian, Bogoliubov--de Gennes spectrum, and topological phase
diagram. Z.~Shi's \href{https://edhar98.github.io/majorana-modes-machine/reports/Zhenming_Shi/report.pdf}{\emph{Finite-Size Physics \& Majorana Edge Modes}} studies
open chains, edge localisation, and Majorana hybridisation. J.~Singh's
\href{https://edhar98.github.io/majorana-modes-machine/reports/Jaskaran_Singh/report.pdf}{\emph{Qubit Encoding (Jordan--Wigner)}} maps the fermionic model to qubits.
Z.~Liu's \href{https://edhar98.github.io/majorana-modes-machine/reports/Zhengyi_Liu/report.pdf}{\emph{Measuring Topology on Circuits (VQE + String Order)}} develops
the variational preparation and non-local edge-string measurement.
D.~Stoev's \href{https://edhar98.github.io/majorana-modes-machine/reports/Dobromir_Stoev/report.pdf}{\emph{NISQ Noise on the Diagnostic}} investigates how realistic device
noise degrades that measured signal.

In this report we focus on \href{https://edhar98.github.io/majorana-modes-machine/reports/Edgar_Harutyunyan/report.pdf}{\emph{Error Taxonomy \& Scaling to Large $L$}}. 
Here and throughout, $L$ denotes the number of sites in the chain.
First, we classify physical perturbations by mapping them to qubit operators
and determine whether they preserve fermion parity and leave the bulk gap open.
Next, we examine how the edge-string diagnostic scales using exact free-fermion references,
fixed-depth variational states, and matrix-product-state (MPS) quantum trajectories under
noise models derived from recorded IBM device calibrations up to $L=100$.
Thus, the two large-system limitations are insufficient circuit expressibility 
below a minimum depth $r^\ast$ and noise accumulated over the entangling gates,
whose number grows linearly with $L$. We identify the shallowest adequately expressive depth 
using exact small-system benchmarks and then test how the edge-string signal survives in calibrated large-$L$ models.
We discuss the protection principle and the large-$L$ limit,
while the channel-by-channel noise mechanics and noisy-optimisation
results are treated in D.~Stoev's \href{https://edhar98.github.io/majorana-modes-machine/reports/Dobromir_Stoev/report.pdf}{\emph{NISQ Noise on the Diagnostic}}.
